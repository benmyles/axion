# Asking about a material the animal has never tested

A familiar grain can stop working after a change of water. In Axion, that change can now come from dissolved material carried by a current. An animal can infer something about the water from other coatings, ask a second animal to test one of those coatings, then give a new answer about the original grain. The question itself, every physical sample, the name the animal speaks and the final meal are observable.

## The physical problem

Each grain has one of four catalytic coatings, and each substrate has two reactive surface groups. Two dissolved components can change which groups are exposed. Three additional components can shear one group's orientation as a function of the other. The operations are reversible. With five components the chemistry can express all 24 one-to-one mappings between the four coatings and the four soils.

Five six-bit quantities fit in one 32-bit word per site. At a water exchange each component travels in proportion to the donor concentration, with seeded integer rounding and a local capacity limit. An animal can move clay, fibre or grains into the path of a current. Those changes alter both transport and dilution. A simple test removes the built banks from a copy of the watershed, advances both copies without animals, and measures where a different coating would now react. In the five-solute glassmarsh run, removing the banks changed the closing reaction law at **349 sites** after 128 ticks, while both landscapes retained identical total inventories of each solute. This measures a physical legacy. Deliberate manufacture of a particular solution remains an open question. [Detached habitat measurements](brine/habitat-measurements.csv)

The inference assay withholds one entire coating. Reference tests use only the other three. The same visible query is presented in two baths with opposite correct choices. A response can depend on past outcomes and on the current grain and soil. The predictive circuit has no receptor for the hidden reaction map, the correct deposit, the test seed or the reference demonstrator's private state. Duplicated animals with frozen synapses necessarily give the same answer in both baths. Every correct choice and every meal is scored using the material simulator.

## What can evolve

Thirty-two possible cell sites share two external sensory channels. A genome specifies which cells fire for each incoming pattern, how many cells are expressed, how each firing state changes after an observed event, what each acquired state contributes to the downstream firing threshold, and how attention weighs potential contacts. The two writable bits of all 32 cells occupy **eight bytes** in two bit planes. A 32-bit truth table in the genome can express an arbitrary two-bit update conditioned on the firing of the cell and on the observed outcome.

The first founder population developed randomly wired sensory masks. The other developmental regime allows two branches to converge on a cell. Each branch chooses two incoming bits and any binary Boolean gate. Its downstream gate can also be any binary Boolean gate. Point mutation, node duplication, receptive-field mutation and recombination can change the expressed pattern. The complete 76-byte genotype records the resulting wiring and inherited rules. Founders receive neither a menu of solvents nor a map that connects the same coatings across different soils.

Positive and negative test outcomes can select different cell states throughout a lifetime. The circuit compares the resulting weighted firing for the two candidate soils. A prospective variant briefly copies the two trace words, advances them once with a visible response and once without one, and evaluates how much each outcome could change the pending choice. Heritable gains can suppress particular sources of evidence, and mutations can reverse the direction of a neural response. A new encounter can also recruit a genetically variable period of sampling before the animal trusts its earlier confidence. The number of attainable chemical transformations, neural primitives, tissue capacity and the social interface are choices of this simulator.

Living watershed residents use these signals in their ordinary somatic afferents, choice of where to press, and construction planner. The number of expressed predictive cells and the size of the somatic spiking brain can both change through descent. Offspring acquire fresh predictive traces. An individual returned to the watershed keeps its own tissue and learned vocabulary.

## Why a change to the environment mattered

In a long-lived patch, a grain can be tried repeatedly. The first populations exploited repetition and usually skipped costly reference samples. In a second ecology the current exposes a thin reference layer before delivering a rich coating that can be tried only once. In the survey habitat, an animal can choose which reference to test. Work consumes energy. Only remaining reserves fund eggs in the provisioned cohort. The foster control gives an equal chance to each adult that could afford an egg. Both controls retain the finite physical food and work accounting. These breeding habitats impose a recognisable ecological pressure, and their germlines can be introduced into the much less constrained watershed.

Four independently seeded populations were raised for 1,024 generations per treatment. Each had 8,192 population slots. They were assessed on 262,144 paired physical choices per row, using seeds outside their training histories. The treatment, age and habitat matter more than the number of repeated test choices when drawing conclusions about evolution.

In a further 1,024 generations, the four survey populations could leave a new reference layer early, and every fourth sampled contact cost additional body energy. Across 262,144 choices, briefly rehearsing both possible observations gave **208,636** productive choices from 1,447,194 reference outcomes. Much of the benefit came from choosing informative experiments generally, with a smaller dependence on the identity of the pending goal. ### Survival in the unstructured watershed

Eight watersheds started directly from random genes, using four seeds in each of the two solvent environments. After 32,768 ticks there were 649–979 residents per habitat. Their predictions on the unseen coating still clustered around chance. The natural watershed permits many ways to survive, including eating already accessible food.

We also allowed provisioned and fostered germlines to compete in freely reproducing watersheds for 8,192 ticks. For each matched pair, the body, somatic brain, ordinary hand, starting reserves and source of every other inherited organ were held shared. Cohorts exchanged positions in a second run. The five-solute introductions ended with **6,693** descendants of provisioned germlines and **3,749** fostered descendants across 16 runs. The two-solute introductions ended with **4,610** and **6,828** respectively. Muting the predictive readouts gave exactly symmetric combined censuses in both chemistries. The effect depends on environment and lineage. There is no consistent general survival advantage. [All 64 introductions](brine/competition.csv)

## Two animals at one physical table

The speaker was born in a populated watershed descended from the shinglebank. Its population received experimentally reared predictive germlines. We exported the highest-energy resident of each of six candidate habitats before seeing the conversation results. Each received a visual-vocabulary school, two fact-question forms and two forms about its own intended action. We chose the released speaker by its validation performance. The subsequent test uses separate seeds. [All six candidates](brine/validation-dialogue.csv)

A tutor establishes the action-question forms by watching the resident make a paid choice and pointing to the site it chose or declined. The resident only gets the public acoustic sequence, the indicated referent, the observed chemistry and its own recent action. Its acquired contradiction contacts distinguish ten possible relationships, including prospective choice. Thirty pointed examples used four training nouns. The four test nouns never occupied that slot during these lessons. Distinguishing a report of an intention from a report of a reaction is important. An animal can correctly report where it will act and be mistaken about what it will get there.

In consultation, the speaker selects a reference sample with a different coating and utters a grounded word naming one of two cups. The listener receives that single waveform, the sample and its own independently permuted scene. It pays for the intervention. The speaker can see which contact actually occurred and whether it produced food. It has no access to the listener's thought, the hidden solvent or the outcome of testing the requested coating. One acoustic shutter closes reference requests and another can close only the final advice.

In the five-solute held-out test, the speaker answered **21,504 questions about its intended action**. With consultation allowed for up to nine contacts, **14,834** answers identified the environmentally appropriate deposit. Closing the request channel gave **10,752**. Inverting the observed outcomes gave **9,612**. All **21,504** spoken reports matched the physical choice of an independently replayed motor twin, including the mistaken choices. The two-solute test gave 15,114 environmentally correct answers in 21,504 questions. The test paired opposite baths at identical tables. An unchanged policy cannot solve both sides.

On factual questions about which soil *actually feeds* the grain, the same animal often abstained. Even with demonstrations, the five-solute test gave 8,868 correct words, 3,114 wrong words and 9,522 silences. A counterfactual with the final deposit word substituted reversed the listener's actions while preserving the speaker's judgment. Seeing references, calibrating confidence, reporting a policy, and having that policy work are independently measurable capabilities. These experiments use a bounded physical world, designed channels and learned question frames. They leave spontaneous language, general conversation and human-level reasoning unproved. [All held-out conditions and working controls](brine/held-dialogue.csv)

## When to ask

The animal can ask without an externally specified sequence of tests. With only the confidence of its previous material state, the released speaker gathered 17,178 reference outcomes across 21,504 five-solute action questions and reached the productive choice 10,842 times. It often arrived at a new table confident in a decision that had made sense in the previous water.

The new sensory event is arrival at a fresh table. The genome can now express a minimum of zero to seven observations of that place before its existing confidence ends a consultation. Each actual visible outcome advances the local count. The same 32-bit attention word, physical budget and synaptic rule continue to control the rest of the visit. The released genome reads as a minimum of three. The occupied memory and learned strengths survive between tables. With this arrival signal, the speaker chose its own experiments and stopping point, gathered 79,024 outcomes and answered **12,640 / 21,504** action questions appropriately. In the paired confidence-only intervention it answered **10,842 / 21,504** appropriately. A full nine demonstrations gave **14,834**. The interpretation of those three developmental bits was added after this animal's original breeding. This comparison demonstrates the effect of the mechanism in the retained genome. [Eight interventions on the same four held-out seeds](brine/arrival-dialogue.csv)

## Execution and storage

The complete writable material organ occupies **84 bytes per resident**, including 76 bytes of inherited wiring and eight bytes of acquired state. Each input or outcome updates up to 32 cells together with Boolean operations on machine words. The habitat adds four bytes of dissolved stocks per site. The checkpoint stores both the ionic inventory and each owner’s learned traces. The native Blackwell executable and a forward-JIT build from `sm_80` PTX produced identical 128 × 128, 1,024-cell-capacity habitat bytes, twelve intervention readings and detached chemical outcomes after 91 ticks.

For deployment, the circuit only has to preserve the relative ordering of four soils for each coating, tie behaviour, and which cues reach firing threshold. Two bits encode the ordinal rank of each of the 16 cues. One bit per cue retains the firing threshold. The result occupies **six bytes per resident**. In a native RTX PRO 2000 Blackwell laptop benchmark, 106,400 readout comparisons across 1,330 residents and 2,097,152 query results agreed with the writable version. Seven timed GPU batches gave medians of 0.0246 ms for the settled image and 0.0799 ms for the writable model. The timed kernels generate query indices on the device, revisit the resident table and write results into device memory. Dataset transfers, record generation and ecological learning are outside this timing.

In the corresponding inhabited 64 × 64 saltmarsh run, advancing 8,388,608 site-ticks took **0.159 seconds**, or **52.7 million site-ticks/s**. The closing 1,330 residents expressed 17,114 predictive cells in addition to their somatic spiking circuits. The independently checked detached exposure changed the reaction map at 155 sites when the existing banks were removed. The single-tick neural intervention changed inputs for four residents and changed zero final motor choices at this census. [Measured GPU batches and whole-habitat rates](brine/performance.csv)

## Reproduce the saltmarsh and the speaker

```sh
make -j2 NATIVE=1 CUDA_ARCH=sm_120
mkdir -p salt-study
./axion floodplain --load examples/shinglebank.flood --brine 2 --brine-plan latent --brine-rear 2048 --brine-shore survey --brine-examples 9 --brine-tides 16 --work-capacity 16384 --steps 2048 --report-every 512 --save salt-study/marsh.flood --export-mind salt-study/born.mind
./axion ground --load salt-study/born.mind --origin random --generations 48 --population 64 --worlds 8 --episodes 1024 --crowd 4 --dialect english --probes 128 --save salt-study/namer.mind
python3 tools/field-language.py --speaker salt-study/namer.mind --save salt-study/speaker.mind --output salt-study/old-chemistry.csv --scenes 1
python3 tools/brine-language.py --speaker salt-study/speaker.mind --save salt-study/surveyor.mind --output salt-study/dialogue.csv --tables 2048
```

In the retained saltmarsh, the construction planner receives predictions from the salt circuit. Use `--brine-plan empirical` to give only that planner the earlier material table while leaving the salt circuit connected to pressing and somatic firing. This change led to a different living population after 2,048 ticks. A saved two-solute environment can acquire new minerals through `--brine 5`. Only the newly introduced components enter from the springs. The other inventories and all existing neural tissue persist.

```sh
python3 tools/brine-study.py --output salt-study/populations
python3 tools/brine-competition.py --donors salt-study/populations --output salt-study/competition.csv
./axion floodplain --load examples/saltmarsh.flood --brine 5 --brine-plan latent --steps 4096 --brine-assay 16 --assay-inquiry --legacy 128 --save salt-study/next.flood
./axion floodplain --load examples/saltmarsh.flood --steps 0 --brine-bench 2097152
make NATIVE=1 CUDA_ARCH=sm_120 test-brine
```

An independent test reads the binary habitat, checks the movement of each solute against the actual water movement, and reconstructs each animal one neuron at a time. It compares the GPU's choices, ties, received observations, work, food, reversals and deducibility in twelve conditions. It also checks restart, expansion, germline transfer, the physical inventory, and equality of writable and frozen decisions. The closed-world deductive oracle is only an auditor.

## Research informing the experiments

A November 2026 human perturbation study measured a causal contribution of the hippocampus to structure-based inference. Its distinction between learning observable transitions and exploiting hidden structure motivated the paired-bath design. A separate study using human intracranial recordings found evidence of coordinated replay during compositional inference. That motivated the experiment in which the material circuit briefly advances its own possible observation states before choosing a test. Both design interpretations are engineering hypotheses. Axion has no anatomical hippocampus. [Human relational-structure intervention](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-026-02627-w) · [Human replay during compositional inference](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-026-02291-3)

Experiments in mice have separated rapid inference of context changes from learning by reward after the new choice. A theoretical study analyzed how the geometry of neural populations affects transfer across tasks with shared latent structure. In Axion, changing which receptor inputs converge provided an experimentally testable counterpart. We evaluated it by physically withholding a whole coating, changing only which observations reached the animal, and measuring whether the resulting action produced food. [Flexible perceptual inference](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41325470/) · [Population geometry and shared structure](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-025-02183-y)
