# A creature recounts its own watershed

At tick 12,271 a wet fibre changed a passing pigment during a release by resident 8,749. When asked later what happened, the resident ended a 68-word account with **“plum went from east to south as jade.”** Its account matched seven successive encounters in its own lifetime, including their colours, locations and order. Asked “What came after plum?”, it described the passage following the first plum arrival. Asked the same question about amber, it stayed silent. The retained history contained no amber arrival. ## From a material event to a phrase

Four local seats hold carriers with independently visible pigments. A moving carrier can exchange pigments with a wet wick before reaching the outlet. Both exchanges conserve the inventory. Clay, pressure, living vegetation, water and fibre already shape the flow and are modified by animals. Each observer sees the resulting light through its own retinal wiring. An observation enters its history only when both changes are detectable. A body turn moves the retinal position addresses. The observer subtracts its own heading before retaining the two positions, preserving a private reference frame across turns. This compact mechanism costs eight bytes per habitat slot for up to seven events. A continuously followed slot also records birth dates, making it possible to exclude every prior occupant. [Material and vocal dynamics](../include/axion/utterance.hpp) · [Independent reconstruction](../tools/utterance-audit.py)

The English-speaking guide learned after extraction. A tutor spoke while the creature observed individual events and pairs. A heard sound supplied an acoustic pattern. The learner received its own sensory activity, without token-to-feature alignments, model answers at test time or an English parser. Its inherited spiking gate and Boolean plasticity program controlled which retinal populations acquired a connection to each vocal phase. Examples could rule out an association, establish a join between clauses or teach the circuit to retain a first or last event. A second set of demonstrations paired questions about prior events with publicly spoken replies. These could establish where a question varied, which part of an observation the variable referred to, the direction of search through memory and which acquired description to say. The same code can learn a reversed convention from another tutor. [Acquired projections](../include/axion/narrative.hpp) · [Temporal retrieval](../include/axion/narrative_recall.hpp) · [External task and tutor](../include/axion/narrative_school.hpp)

The classroom supplies seven-event cases after tuition on one- and two-event descriptions. The starting-place and incoming-pigment combinations in the descriptive exam are held out throughout descriptive tuition. Separate temporal lessons use longer histories, with only amber and jade appearing as the variable in the question. Plum and teal occur elsewhere in the creature's experience and appear in those question positions for the first time at test. Changing only the name demands a different response in many pairs. Absence, ambiguity, dim light and failure to learn can all leave the creature silent. [Held-out speech](learned-descriptions/held-out-speech.csv) · [Point-by-point retrieval](learned-descriptions/temporal-retrieval.csv)

## What the tests distinguished

| Observation or intervention | Result |
| --- | --- |
| The guide described 256 unfamiliar seven-event histories, each presented in one of two orders | 256 exact full reports and 768 exact answers to the three narrower prompts |
| Two new names in temporal questions on 512 fresh layouts | All 1,685 answerable questions located the correct event. All 363 questions with no target elicited silence |
| Both new names required different answers on the same history | Both answers were correct in all 643 such pairs |
| An examiner exchanged the two names reaching the ear while grading the original questions | 0 of 835 intended episodes located. Wording happened to coincide on 32 |
| Public answers were paired with the wrong witnessed histories during the temporal lesson | 0 of 835 intended episodes located, with the same 3,081 units of teaching expenditure |
| A different tutor used the opposite temporal convention | 837 of 837 answerable questions located correctly |
| The spiking gate was held shut | 0 correct descriptions and 0 episodes located |
| A tutor supplied isolated descriptive units alone, shifted the scenes or shuffled its words | Each treatment produced 0 exact seven-event test responses |
| A fresh copy learned each of two other surface conventions | All 1,024 responses were exact in each. One convention reversed clause order, the other could elide repeated beginnings |
| The trained guide encountered captured coastal watercourses | All 1,024 responses were exact on those physical outlet patterns |
| The guide observed ordinary backgrounds in place of high-contrast exposure | 16 of 256 complete histories were described correctly. In a separate temporal test it located 48 of 419 answerable episodes |

Each descriptive test also includes an auditory receiver and a separate CUDA food-release kernel. The two ends of each event have independent physical catches. Reversing the incoming speech eliminated the catches in the seven-event test. Exchanging the receiver's two motor axes also eliminated catches while preserving its spoken descriptions. For the batch receiver probe the tutor supplies a full report unless another hearing condition is selected. This matters in dim light, where the guide can miss events that the tutor still describes. The interactive curtain experiment uses audio actually emitted by the resident. ## Birth, hearing and inheritance

The unseeded colony supplied 1,084 living candidates born after tick 9,216, each with at least five retained field events and the organs needed to participate. Each received the same temporary classroom experience. Of those candidates, 372 gave all 32 requested answers correctly. The guide was the first top-scoring candidate in a predetermined traversal. It was born at tick 11,577, reached generation 54 and expressed 64 somatic spiking cells by extraction. The field stores acquired speech projections only for particular schooled residents. Returning the guide associates those contacts with the same living genome and birth record, while preserving its own field history. A newborn gets a fresh history. [Every enrolled resident](learned-descriptions/screen.tsv.gz) · [Continuable taught colony](../examples/brook-watershed.flood.zst)

The guide then taught the first twelve other qualifying graduates of that screen. Each new pupil observed the stream through its own eyes. All spoken answers during tuition, including the temporal answers, came from the guide's acquired circuit. The examiner selected the questions and test cases. Four pupils located all 420 answerable events each, two located 342, and six located none. The public pupil occupied slot 9,194, was born at tick 11,812 and belonged to generation 70. It subsequently described all 128 complete held-out histories. These are relatives from one watershed and performance varies even among residents selected for descriptive ability. [Twelve pupils](learned-descriptions/transmission.csv) · [Peer-produced answers](learned-descriptions/questions-from-guide.tsv.gz) · [Uncoached pupil](../examples/brook-pupil-young.mind)

English tuition and naturally occurring calls are separate experiences. In the living watershed, inherited vocal resonators can emit between one and eight syllables in response to a change. Their taps, mixing, register, termination, receptive tissue and plasticity vary genetically. A neighbor's hearing changes its spiking state. When it can also see the event, existing auditory contacts can associate sounds with its own features. Later it can commit to a location and a pigment before using that encounter to update its ear. It can eat only from available material. In 4,096 ticks the unseeded colony paid for 692,963 syllables. Receivers obtained 1,146 food units through this pathway, including 666 during incomplete visual exposure. Reversing the hearing order in the corresponding watershed yielded 327 such units. These runs diverge after their first different actions. [Living field controls](learned-descriptions/field-controls.csv)

In a separate common nursery, descent drew on local food and the energy left after vocal work. Three food-selected lineages and three uniformly sampled comparisons ran for 160 generations of 1,024 individuals. The selected lineage from seed 92,581 produced signals that yielded 378 upstream and 368 downstream catches out of 384 opportunities per end. Its uniformly sampled comparison yielded 14 and 378. Another selected lineage captured 382 upstream and only 24 downstream. Wild deployment also changed the ordering of the lineages' performances. These populations show several viable specializations and substantial dependence on the environment. ## When a neighbor changes the caller’s own voice

The optional dialect environment connects existing auditory contacts back to the caller's vocal phases. A hereditary mask opens individual phases to that input. Their thresholds and reachable registers are also heritable. A previously heard pitch can win when its auditory contacts respond specifically to the current visual features. It then changes what the creature actually emits and pays for. Newborns can learn from any audible neighbor. The field supplies neither a vocabulary nor a teacher assignment. The local water, motor and hearing interactions run in staggered two-by-two neighborhoods, a cellular-automaton technique that keeps most communication on the GPU local. The recurrent brains and inherited growth programs can vary across residents. [Auditory feedback to the voice](../include/axion/utterance.hpp) · [Field speech and catches](../src/flood_ink.cuh)

In a fresh unseeded watershed, auditory tissue changed **22,748 of 205,640 paid calls**, altering 34,863 syllables. An independently graded set of field samples includes the caller's pre-release auditory rows, inherited vocal output, received visual pattern and actual emitted sequence. Every sampled sequence was reproduced from those inputs. In the seeded watershed from lineage 30,757 the same mechanism altered 41,496 calls. Differences in paid output establish that acquired tissue participated in production. They leave open how long an individual convention persists in unrestricted competition. [All four living populations](learned-descriptions/acquired-field-calls.csv) · [Raw auditory and optical tissue](learned-descriptions/wild-calls.tsv.gz) · [Resume the dialect watershed](../examples/brook-dialects.flood.zst)

A matched experiment isolated transmission. Two founders produced calls differing by one pitch within each register. At each link, two fresh pupils with the same inherited voice and blank ears heard their respective predecessors over the same 64 physical scenes. Those pupils became the next link's callers. On each of 256 independent chains, both pupils were then shown eight held-out events. Six links later, the pupils produced **different calls on 2,045 of 2,048 identical pictures**. Listening with the corresponding founder’s calibrated ear yielded 2,048 of 8,192 available food portions. Exchanging the ears yielded 12. Disconnecting auditory feedback eliminated all between-pupil differences and gave 2,048 portions in either arrangement. Thus the learned convention changed who could interpret a signal while its aligned catch equaled the disconnected control. [All links, treatments and catches](learned-descriptions/vocal-transmission.csv) · [One row per chain and link](learned-descriptions/transmission-paired.tsv.gz)

Misaligning sound and sight during learning reduced the sixth-link difference to 621 pictures. In another treatment the second founder used an independently evolved vocal organ. In that case the sixth-link pupils differed on only 185 pictures, and either choice of receiving ear captured 4,096 portions. The final calls also frequently differed from either founder’s original call. These six-link experiments isolate social inheritance using genetically matched learners. The naturally born field inhabitants above undergo reproductive selection and have varied genotypes. [Mispaired rearing](learned-descriptions/transmission-crossed.tsv.gz) · [Disconnected vocal feedback](learned-descriptions/transmission-cut.tsv.gz) · [Paired CUDA assay](../src/chorus.cuh)

The contrast persisted through **24 successive learners**. Across three fresh seed conditions the two pupils made different sounds for 1,966, 1,973 and 1,994 of 2,048 identical pictures at the final link. A call also carried information about which picture was present. Keeping the same receiver and complete set of utterances, but exchanging the calls between successive scenes, reduced the three aligned catches from 2,048, 2,047 and 2,042 to 310, 223 and 274. Disconnecting auditory feedback preserved event-sensitive inherited sounds while eliminating every difference between the two rearing histories. Each comparison pairs 256 rearing chains within its seed. ## Execution on Blackwell

The learning, field encounters, mutable spiking circuitry, calls, hearing, production, inference, breeding, physical release and retained history all execute on CUDA. The host manages checkpoints, experiments and visualization. A compile pass checks each vocal phase against all 256 possible sensory volleys for its active clause shape. For this guide it proved 23 of 25 active selectors equivalent to a four-entry register lookup. Two projections needed residual plastic cells. The resulting 616-byte production image can generate the same utterances without reading the 4,848-byte learning tissue. An optional shared dictionary accelerates repeated requests for the same speaker. The dictionary-backed path uses 8,736 bytes of production data. Prompt recognition, writable questions, audition, whole-animal state and allocator overhead are additional. [GPU compiler and residuals](../include/axion/narrative_image.hpp) · [Production kernels](../src/narrative.cuh)

The median of three independent batches on the RTX PRO 2000 Blackwell laptop was **419.5 million seven-event requests/s** for the 616-byte image, versus **347.6 million/s** for direct evaluation and **452.5 million/s** for the dictionary. Each batch processed 1,048,576 different inputs nine timed times after warm-up. Each input asked one of four learned questions, which produce answers of different lengths. Each lane produced a hash of its actual words. Every compiled result matched direct evaluation. Timing covers vocal production on a shared speaker and excludes the preparation of the learned image, physical simulation and interactive latency. [Per-seed CUDA event timings](learned-descriptions/vocal-inference.csv)

A separate 1,048,576-site estuary advanced from tick 414,104 to **416,152**. At the final census it contained **327,221 creatures and 21,021,376 expressed spiking cells**. The measured advance covered 2,147,483,648 site-ticks in 40.737 seconds, or **52.72 million site-ticks/s**. Including census overhead, that run took 44.485 seconds. The optical kernel with per-event tracing disabled used 150 registers and zero local bytes per thread in this native build. Across that entire sparse estuary, listeners obtained no food through the new call pathway in this interval. The smaller watershed was much richer in vision and interaction. An earlier recording used a shared flag position for two separate auditory controls and took 78.134 seconds. We separated the controls and reran the complete interval. ## Research and limits

Children exposed to miniature languages can use the way words group and recur to learn phrase structure. A separate cross-situational study found evidence of learning syntax in eight- and nine-year-olds even when evidence of vocabulary learning was weak. Those findings motivated distinct tests for event words, clause grouping and transfer to new referents. They supply experimental questions rather than a benchmark equating the simulator to a child. [Schuler and colleagues, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1080/15475441.2026.2724818) · [Cross-situational acquisition of vocabulary and grammar](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305000925100135)

Human recordings found activity before speech that encoded properties of upcoming words. Head-direction experiments in rats separately showed how self-motion can update a direction signal in different reference frames. The engineering choices here are much smaller in scope. The vocal image compiles a previously acquired production program, and the four-direction observer uses exact proprioceptive heading. [Human word production](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aem7976) · [Head-referenced updating](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-026-10086-2)

Two 2026 zebra-finch studies illustrate why acoustic resemblance and cultural inheritance merit separate measurements. One identified a synaptic site involved in imitative song learning. Another raised colonies with acoustically constrained founders and followed which song features persisted and diversified. In Axion we score exact founder imitation, divergence between pupils and successful physical interpretation separately. [Synaptic locus of song learning](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10510-x) · [Song cultures in controlled colonies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41891168/)

The experimental language has four places, four pigments, a bounded sound inventory and seven-event working memory. The available temporal search paths, the visual feature populations, the external lesson generator and the capacity limits are designed into the system. We have evidence for grounded recombination, a small form of event reference and transmission between some individuals. In an ordinary-language interview it answered the two familiar questions about its history and returned “?” to all seven other questions. These asked for a cause, a counterfactual, a frequency, a construction plan, a proposed experiment, another animal's view and a teaching strategy. Unrestricted conversation, learning a new field of knowledge on request, novel abstract concepts and human-level cognition are still open.
