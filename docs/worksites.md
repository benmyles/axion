# Learning to work the shingle

One resident sampled every combination of four mineral surfaces and four soils during its life in the watershed. After separate language tuition, it could tell another animal which deposit would feed a previously untested grain. The listener heard one word and spent its own energy to test the grain at the named deposit.

```sh
./axion talk --speaker examples/shingle-witness.mind --listener examples/shore-reader.mind
```

Enter the following lines in the conversation. The words identify visible objects in this particular scene. A different table assigns the objects fresh appearances and positions.

```text
/assay-table 4001
which soil feeds hare?
which soil resists hare?
```

The speaker says **newt** to the first question and **vole** to the second. The listener goes to sites 6 and 9 respectively, collecting two food units and zero. Enter `/listen-field foil` and repeat the first question. The speaker still says **newt**. The listener hears the other deposit's name, moves to site 9 and collects zero. Use `/listen-field open` and `/field-memory blank` to test the same question after silencing only the speaker's acquired soil contacts.

## What an animal can change

In the original driftbank only 141 of 1,953 grains lay on the ground. Vacant hands held 1,175. Grains can now fall out of a vacant hand at the next available local contact. Animals can compress a grain against another grain, pay to excite it, test an exposed deposit, eat the product and attend to what happened. Their own bodies, sense organs, preceding motor volley, inherited sensory gates and acquired consequences influence which contacts they sample. A creature can live an entire life without trying a particular action.

A ground site can keep the four most recent material observations and the tick of its newest observation. A mark records the two physical surfaces, the type of interaction, whether there was a response and whether an animal made it. Water can scour marks. A second animal reads them through its own sensory mapping and revises its own contacts. Experiments that restrict a hatchling to animal-made, prebirth evidence require the newest mark at a site to predate that hatchling, or the site to have a provably earlier origin in a transplant.

Reactive terrain binds a finite amount of organic material. Its capacity is four units plus contributions from local clay, banks and exposed mineral groups. Moving a bank or a grain can change that capacity and release carbon as litter. A matching charged surface can free up to its contact-area allowance of bound material. Releasing *k* carbon units consumes one unit of excitation and produces *k* + 1 food units. Body reserves supply deliberate excitation. Solar input, storage, work, released carbon and heat have separate ledgers.

These are local CUDA updates. Alternating disjoint contacts let animals and materials change both sides of a face without a global lock. Each resident adds 24 bytes of inquiry tissue, including eight bytes of heritable attention and impulse genes. Each site adds eight bytes for imprints and, where enabled, two for bound carbon. The hand genome controls the two-bit learning transitions and which contact states release a pulse. Parents pass genes, and hatchlings acquire their own contact states. Body form, spiking population size, routing, expressed update instructions and sensory gains can also change through descent. The simulator still specifies the available operations, sensor channels, material conservation laws and memory capacities.

## A field curriculum with no prescribed construction

The first cohort came from the inhabited driftbank. An earlier experiment had introduced a hand **genotype** selected for material learning. No constructor was shown a target arrangement during these field runs. After 512 ticks of active investigation there were 705 living residents, 1,610 grains on the ground and 26 in vacant hands. The construction test sampled 768 resident copies with replacement and independently laid eight beds per copy. It tested cold-start production after removing the builder. A second exposure checked each individually indispensable displaced part.

| Stored experience at the first census | Productive beds / 6,144 | Correct soil choices / 6,144 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Resident's own experience | 104 | 3,530 |
| All acquired material and hand contacts cleared | 2 | 3,152 |
| Local ground reread with the resident's own hands | 127 | 3,867 |
| Same ground, outcomes rotated among marks of each local type | 57 | 3,546 |

Of the 104 field-experienced builds, 101 needed at least two displaced components. Untouched and insulated twins produced nothing. The local reread is a separate intervention with a deliberately broad four-by-four window. In the closing mixed-sediment watershed, residents made only 12 productive builds in 6,144 tests. Erasing only their motor contacts **increased** this to 24. The data do not support a steady rise in construction skill from one census to the next. [All eight interventions at each census](worksites/field-transfer.csv)

We also transplanted newly hatched residents into four common gardens. The parents were absent, direct manipulation and site planning were suppressed, and only animals exactly 16 ticks old entered the test. Intact and shuffled gardens supplied 15,906 readings of animal-made marks older than the reader. The washed gardens supplied zero. Across 65,536 trials per condition, juveniles chose the reactive soil 33,244 times with intact marks, 32,975 times with locally shuffled outcomes and 32,778 times with washed ground. The corresponding working builds were 12, 11 and 12. Marks carried a little usable information at that age. Construction still depended on other experience. ### Populations with random founders

Four independent founder seeds began with random bodies, brains, hands and learning genes. A first paired series ran for 8,192 ticks on loose material. A second series ran for 32,768 ticks on bound sediment with either learned or unbiased selection of where to press. Nothing in these runs selected the winners of a knowledge test for reproduction. Every birth occurred in the watershed.

| Bound sediment | Living at the closing censuses | Correct soil choices / 65,536 | Productive builds | Same individuals with experience erased |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Learned choice of press location | 78–642 | 30,934 | 431 | 368 |
| Unbiased choice of press location | 436–1,013 | 31,520 | 1,490 | 1,165 |

One unbiased population produced 1,211 working builds on 16,384 beds. Its blank copies still produced 950. Other populations performed worse after experience. The independent unit for an evolutionary claim is a founder habitat. Thousands of probes of related animals do not establish a general advantage across founders. A failed early sediment design trapped 1,892,346 units of carbon after 1,024 ticks and the population died out. Finite binding capacity made viable alternatives possible. ## What the conversation measures

At tick 15,744 the shinglebank contained 1,105 residents, 136 expressed body plans and 295 distinct expressed spiking programs. The sampling record includes both direct manipulation and observations read from material traces. We chose the animal with the most sampled soil pairs, breaking ties by sampled terminal pairs, age and then lowest slot. This uses exposure only. Slot 1608 was 483 ticks old and had sampled all 16 soil pairs and all 16 terminal pairs. An independent decoder found eight residents with an entirely correct soil response table. The retained speaker had 64 active somatic spiking cells and four inherited modules.

The visual vocabulary was externally schooled, using a separately selected grounding law over 48 generations and a circuit exposed to 1,024 captioned scenes. Its chemical memory came from the watershed. The resident subsequently paid for duplicate physical coupon tests. A tutor pointed out examples of two question forms. The animal received 26 examples with *moth*, *vole*, *newt* and *wren*. The held-out questions used *hare*, *seal*, *lark* and *toad*. Those words belonged to its visual vocabulary but had never occupied the noun slot in any taught material question.

The acoustic circuit can store four question frames with up to eight pulses each. A demonstrated referent fixes a noun slot. The hereditary transition law updates contradictory evidence for eight available interpretations, including proximity and reactive or unreactive material. When hearing an unfamiliar combination, the speaker uses the noun, the learned relation and its wild-acquired material table. It can utter one grounded deposit name or remain silent. The recipient gets only that pulse and its own independently ordered scene. Both animals can be inspected separately. This is a bounded generalization experiment with designed perceptual and grammatical primitives. Spontaneous language, unrestricted conversation and child-level reasoning remain open.

| Channel or memory intervention | Correct names on 54 nameable questions | Listener chose intended site | Productive requests yielding food / 27 | Requests for inactive soil yielding food / 27 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Intact | 54 | 54 | 27 | 0 |
| Other deposit's word substituted | 54 | 0 | 0 | 27 |
| Sound shuttered | 54 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Speaker's soil memory cleared | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The study offered 48 new tables and asked 70 held-out questions per condition. Sixteen questions lacked a uniquely nameable answer. The intact animal stayed silent on all sixteen. The memory-erased animal stayed silent on all seventy. A silent recipient spent zero units. The speaker's acquired vocabulary and question frames survive reintroduction into the watershed and checkpoint/reload for the rest of its life. A sparse, ownership-checked record associates that large vocabulary with its living owner. Newborns do not inherit the parent's speech record. ## Execution on Blackwell

The mixed-sediment run advanced 16,777,216 site-ticks at **56.36 million site-ticks/s** on the RTX PRO 2000 Blackwell laptop GPU. Its 512-cell capacity and complete suite of ecological organs required 967 bytes of per-site arrays, plus shared books and runtime buffers. A separate 128 × 128 frontier with capacity for 1,024 somatic cells per resident advanced 33,554,432 site-ticks in 0.426 seconds, or **78.83 million site-ticks/s**. It ended with 6,270 living residents. Density, world size, enabled organs and other laptop work affect throughput.

For a frozen animal, collapse the 16 terminal, 16 soil, 32 motor and four basal odor readouts into two word planes and one byte. One warp advances a 32-site six-step spatial forecast. The native benchmark compared all 75,140 receptive outputs of 1,105 animals with their writable tissues. Each of 524,288 held-out diagrams also matched. Seven interleaved CUDA-event timings gave medians of 0.538 ms for the nine-byte image and 0.577 ms for the 40-byte writable structures. At 1,048,576 diagrams, the settled kernel reached **977.5 million diagrams/s**. Native Blackwell machine code and a forward-JIT PTX build also reproduced the same 128 × 128 habitat bytes and all eight transfer conditions after a separate 97-tick test. These timing measurements cover a settled inference kernel or the physical advance as indicated. Living animals retain writable state to learn, move, reproduce and update their terrain. ## Reproduce

```sh
make -j2 NATIVE=1 CUDA_ARCH=sm_120
python3 tools/worksite-study.py --output ./field-study
./axion floodplain --load field-study/finite-mixed.flood --steps 0 --slot 1608 --export-mind field-study/born.mind
./axion ground --load field-study/born.mind --origin random --generations 48 --population 64 --worlds 8 --episodes 1024 --crowd 4 --dialect english --probes 128 --save field-study/namer.mind
python3 tools/field-language.py --speaker field-study/namer.mind --listener examples/shore-reader.mind --save field-study/witness.mind --output field-study/dialogue.csv
./axion floodplain --load examples/shinglebank.flood --steps 0 --worksite-bench 524288 --assay-inquiry
make NATIVE=1 CUDA_ARCH=sm_120 test-worksites
```

For ordinary continuation, run `./axion floodplain --load examples/shinglebank.flood --steps 4096 --save next.flood`. A checkpoint retains the previous hand signal used for attention. The historical study explicitly rests that signal on its first observation when introducing the new physical environment with `--rest-hands`. All later choices use the animal's actual preceding volley. The independent regression decodes habitat bytes, sums every material and energy reservoir, reconstructs each of eight intervention conditions, and checks uninterrupted versus resumed trajectories and speech retention.

## Research informing the design

A 2026 naturalistic study of 31 children documented frequent object manipulation during the development of tool use. Our engineering inference was to make materials accessible and allow repeated, individually chosen actions long before an animal can complete a useful construction. A capuchin study separately manipulated raw stone properties and evidence of earlier use. That motivated a matched intervention that removes the informational relation in local marks while preserving their frequency. Axion's physical symbols and learning laws are deliberately simplified. [Child observations in iScience](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13542482/) · [Primate study and experimental design](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi%3A10.5061/dryad.wm37pvn38)

Human single-neuron recordings show that representations of stimuli and latent context change as people learn to make inferences. Recent hippocampal stimulation work tests a causal contribution to structure-based inference. These results motivate separating a learned relation, its current referent and an independent readout of the consequence. Axion's present held-out-noun test measures reuse of an acquired relation. The harder test is discovering a new relation or hidden environmental law from sparse experience.
