# When should a spiking animal replace a memory?

A brook juvenile learned a change in the underlying pressure thresholds, then used its new model to answer questions about unseen apparatus. In 2,048 episodes after transfer it reconstructed the entire six-input response in 2,028, answered 1,494 of 1,495 answerable questions, and collected food in all 1,018 solvable living-channel layouts. A copy that retained old signs experienced exactly the same movements and feedback. It reconstructed the whole law in zero episodes and still collected food in 1,017. A simple meal count would have missed nearly the entire difference. ## A natural experiment in memory retention

A pressure contact stores positive and negative spike evidence separately. Four inherited truth-table bits specify the next state of each sign as a function of its old state and the new evidence. A separate spiking release gate can veto either update. Table 10 copies incoming evidence. Table 14 retains either old or incoming evidence. The difference is one bit governing what happens when a previously present sign stops arriving.

We sampled 2,048 living juveniles with eyes and limbs per watershed using a seeded scan, excluding animals born more than 2,048 ticks earlier. Each faced eight continuous histories of ten episodes in an independent common garden. They began with empty material contacts and retained them over all ten episodes. At episode six, the pressure thresholds could change. Within each family the relative effectiveness of wick and film could also reverse independently. The animal could watch and physically investigate. Neither transition provided a label. Grades required all 64 projected responses, including configurations the animal had never tested. The classroom score never fed back into wild reproduction. The original animal chose every investigation. Its counterfactual partner received the same movements, times, expenditure and real outcomes in the same order. Only the pressure update bit changed in the partner during the assay. On unchanged thresholds the difference disappeared in these two groups. In a fresh examination, two natural table-10 juveniles reconstructed the new law on all 896 later episodes. Both subsequently learned only 566 of 2,332 answerable spoken items. A naturally occurring table-14 juvenile from the older watershed answered all 2,332 while failing the broader physical transfer. These four selected specimens establish the dissociation in identifiable individuals. ## Separate pressure and acoustic contacts

Axion can now read four bits of the routing gene from the neural module innervating the animal’s tactile band. These bits mask the base update table at pressure contacts. Acoustic contacts keep the base table. All 16 pressure tables are encodable with each of the 16 acoustic tables. The choice depends on the animal’s actual genome and expressed bands. Zero selects the common rule. This uses existing genes and adds zero persistent bytes per creature to the adaptive pathway. Its short material history still takes 12 bytes. The sensory boundary and four-bit table representation are engineered. Evolution can change both tables, the selected module and the release gates.

In mouse motor cortex, individual dendritic compartments showed different relationships between spine activity, neuronal output and plasticity during learning. A 2026 computational study tested how dendritic gating could preserve overlapping assemblies. These findings motivated a narrower engineering hypothesis in Axion. Sharing evidence between senses need not require sharing the same local retention rule. Neither study establishes the particular four-bit implementation. [Wright and colleagues, Science 2025](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ads4706) · [Miękus and Gjorgjieva, Neuron 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2026.07.028)

The routing bits were chosen before the population screen. Enabling their expression immediately reduced the brook’s count of complete learners from 279 to seven. Only 19 of the 2,048 expressed acoustic table 14 and pressure table 10. All seven complete brook learners were among them. Across the brook, older and slow-pulse nurseries, 14 animals with this combination passed the full transfer screen. All 14 received the same acoustic tuition. Each answered 888 of 888 answerable held-out items. Tuition used separate episodes and two pigments. The speech test used unseen layouts, handle arrangements and two other pigments. ## Speaking after the world changes

A second examiner kept each animal alive across 256 histories of 16 episodes. Halfway through, it changed the pressure thresholds. After each episode it brought a separate apparatus to the animal and asked one of six questions. The equipment and question type were drawn independently of the mineral. The questioner saw the displayed before-and-after pigments. The animal’s ears received the words. An independent CUDA grader physically operated the possible interventions to establish which answers were supported. An offline scalar verifier reconstructs the evidence and scores the resulting words. A separate task tested the animal’s construction on actual geometry sampled from a living watershed. ## What survived free reproduction

The slow and continued brook populations each advanced 32,768 ticks with unconstrained mating, feeding and handling. The coast advanced 1,024 ticks. Two arms started from each exact same checkpoint. One expressed the afferent-specific tables and one used the common table throughout. Juveniles were sampled after this free breeding. Subsequent acoustic tuition was external.

| Habitat | Complete learners with common rule | Complete learners with afferent-specific rule | New juveniles tested per arm |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Continued brook | 443 | 52 | 2,048 |
| Slow-pulse nursery | 257 | 56 | 2,048 |
| Coast | 0 | 0 | 2,048 |

Five of the 56 complete slow-pulse juveniles had acoustic table 14 and pressure table 10. All five were tutored. Four answered all 888 answerable school questions and the fifth answered 796. The first qualifying animal in the preregistered scan, slot 13000, was born at tick 53,109 in generation 182. It is the free-born animal in the table above. In a separate 1,024-case instruction exam its independently schooled listener physically completed all 1,022 solvable channels, including all 587 with spoken procedures longer than the training examples. The coastal regional run closed at 315,752 living creatures and 20,280,992 conducting cells at 55.83 million site-ticks per simulation second on the Blackwell laptop. Paired free runs share their start, then births and landscapes diverge. One trajectory per treatment does not establish a universal fitness advantage.
