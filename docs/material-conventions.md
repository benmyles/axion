# Two meanings for the same mark

Two brooksiders can leave a message in a piece of their environment. One sees which feeder contains food and can pulse a wet junction. It leaves. A second animal tests the junction and chooses a feeder. The sender never hears the receiver’s choice during a test. Only the physical state of two pigments connects the two turns.

Two independent lifetimes of the same pair developed opposite conventions. In one lifetime the sender pumped for the left feeder. In the other it pumped for the right. On 2,048 new balanced choices, each reunited pair found food 1,977 times. Exchanging the receivers reduced both scores to 71. Erasing the physical mark gave all four pairs exactly 1,024. We ranked them by the smaller number of lifetimes acquiring either convention, then by their total reliable lifetimes and held-out correct choices. This pair had 13 reliable lifetimes in each direction. Reliability required correctly distinguishing both cue locations with a consistent mark on at least 48 of 64 paired tests. We took the first qualifying lifetime for each meaning, numbered 0 and 1. Only then did we run the final test on a new noise seed and 1,024 drawn fixtures. Every test fixture differed from the training fixture. Draws can recur across the test. In a separate population born under afferent-specific plasticity, slots 5194 and 6127 scored 1,943 with their familiar convention and 105 with the exchanged receiver. ## How a junction holds a bit

The existing cellular pressure field already has a surface coating and a mobile pigment, each with four possible values. A mismatch in their low polarity bit exchanges which material favours wetting. A high-valence coating facing a low-valence carrier reverses the sill threshold. A passing pulse exchanges the bound and mobile pigments. This coupling lets one pressure event change the response to a later event with exactly the same input. It uses zero additional stored bits per habitat site.

The device tried all 32 geologies, 16 ordered pigment pairs and 64 local inputs twice. The first pulse changed the effective law in 5,184 of 32,768 cases. In 448 it passed and closed the junction to the repeated input. Our assay samples physically capable junctions from this set. On an interactive bench the recorded sequence was pass, block, block, pass, pass, block. The fourth pour used a different handle configuration to prime the same trapped pair. All six pours left the other 31 portions in stock. [All material contacts](material-conventions/catalytic-contacts.tsv.gz) · [Six actual pours](material-conventions/sealed-junction.txt)

Free coastal creatures experienced 134,217,728 local pressure updates over 1,024 ticks. Holding the actual row, net and food stock fixed, the chemistry enabled 211,857 pulses, prevented 454,837 and let 39 grips change the pulse when the same grips on inert material would have had no effect. It enabled 77 immediate food portions and cost 63. A subsequent 128-by-128 camera found **256 actual consecutive closures** with an unchanged input and both pigment identities conserved from the preceding beat. None of those 256 sampled first beats involved a handle movement. The camera downloaded 10,244 bytes of count and event data from a 67,108,864-byte device ring. At the last census 313,646 animals had 20,141,952 conducting cells. The habitat advanced at 55.94 million site-ticks per second. ## How the animals acquire a convention

The food cue, pump and two feeder movements are available motor and sensory primitives. The meaning of pumping receives no assignment. Bodies differ genetically in optical access, local learning tables, somatic spike programs, exploratory drive and how they read positive and negative food contacts. The visitor observes the pressure indicator, without seeing the sender, the food location or the pigment identities. The matched receiver receives identical somatic state and motor noise for both possible food locations. The no-mark condition therefore provides an exact one-half baseline even for strongly biased animals.

Each successful choice provides one portion to each forager. Each updates its own active sensory and motor contact through its inherited two-input truth table and somatic release gate. The acquired weights stay separate from acoustic associations. In the final test the weights are frozen. The experiment imposes separated visits, a binary food problem and shared reward during acquisition. A chemical sign on this task establishes one bit of learned communication. The free coastal observations establish natural material memory. They do not establish spontaneous conventions between free coastal animals.

### GPU representation

The signalling organ has four possible optic–motor pairs. Each needs two signed contact bits. If inherited receptors disconnect, two addresses can become indistinguishable. The packed representation preserves those aliases while updating all four pairs in **one byte per animal**. The generic version required 16 bytes per animal for the same contacts. Five interleaved measurements on the Blackwell laptop gave median kernel times of 9.81 versus 13.04 ms in the brook and 9.74 versus 13.00 ms in the slow-pulse population. Each launch trained 2,048 pairs for 512 opportunities in 32 independent lives and tested both possible cues before and after training. Every per-animal result was byte-identical. These timings measure the signalling assay. Whole-habitat speed appears in the watershed table. [Five alternating measurements and receipt hashes](material-conventions/packed-timing.csv)

A November 2026 study described community differences in how chimpanzees used an existing signal. An ant study found that private experience with a food location did not reliably change trail-following in the conditions tested. In Axion the corresponding engineering questions are whether the same act can acquire different uses and how to distinguish learned social use from simply responding to a physical stimulus. Swapping two receivers while keeping their genomes and apparatus identical directly tests the first question. ## Unpaired foraging in the open watershed

The same one-byte contacts can be expressed by ordinary free animals using `--chemical-foraging live`. Their genomes and current motor state determine whether they open a pumping or receiving port. A pumper senses its own local food concentration and can spend energy to pressurize an existing wet site. A receiver senses existing upstream pressure and can cast its net. Each learns from its own actual intake. This field supplies neither a teacher nor paired left/right targets. Birth clears the acquired byte. Returning a taught individual restores its own contacts, and checkpoints preserve them through restart or frontier expansion. The three free habitats also have matched fixed-contact and disconnected treatments.

The diagnostic replay held the same body, spiking state, terrain, motor noise and available resources. Clearing only its appetitive byte isolates the immediate effect of its acquired signs. The coast gives a counterexample to a uniform benefit. A second recording follows an actual pump-enabled wave one pressure beat into the next animal. It holds the recipient’s observed bed and other upstream input fixed when removing the source. In the first brook window eleven waves reached distinct active receivers. Five changed a receiver’s movement, and two of those responses disappeared when the receiver’s own learned contacts were cleared. The corresponding slow-pulse window recorded fourteen arrivals, five changed movements, one learned source and one learned receiver. The learned source and learned receiver occurred in separate events. ## Can the meaning survive turnover?

An established sender or receiver was reunited with 2,048 separately sampled real juveniles in each population. Its learned contacts were held fixed while the novice could adapt. Each novice experienced both founding conventions in separate matched lives. Reliability required the same direction of communication to pass at least 48 of 64 two-cue tests in at least six of eight histories.

| Population | Juvenile receivers that learned either sender’s meaning | Juvenile senders that learned either receiver’s meaning |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Brook | 1,371 / 2,048 | 556 / 2,048 |
| Slow-pulse descendants | 672 / 2,048 | 655 / 2,048 |
| Coastal descendants | 8 / 2,048 | 5 / 2,048 |

None of the three populations met the two-convention criterion when the metabolic feedback was unrelated to the feeder reached. In an eight-replacement chain, even the pre-screened brook recruits lost the distinction by the fifth to sixth replacements. The final readers scored 1,264 of 2,048 with either founding group, including the exchanged group. A researcher observing only that above-baseline score could mistake a re-learned common preference for preservation of the founding convention. ### Overlapping partners

We then replaced 12 individuals, alternating new senders and receivers. A population with four places for each role retained older residents until the fifth member of that role entered. Each newcomer still received exactly 1,024 food opportunities. Its partners rotated evenly. By the final exam both founders and the first four pupils had left. We tested all remaining sender–receiver combinations in each community and all crossings between the two founding conventions.

| Final community, same pre-screened births | Matched founding schools | Exchanged founding schools | Erased mark |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| One adult per role | 730 / 1,024 | 730 / 1,024 | 512 / 1,024 |
| Four adults per role | 9,715 / 16,384 | 7,133 / 16,384 | 8,192 / 16,384 |

Multiple partners preserved a measurable difference in this selected group. The group using the first eligible births without aptitude screening produced 4,077 of 8,192 correct for every pairing of founding schools at width four. The environments, genetic starting points, food budgets and test sites are explicit. The turnover schedule and individual selection rule are experimental.
