# Learning what an action produces

A 784-byte creature can handle an object before it has any use for what the object produces. After tasting a food elsewhere, it can use that experience to change how it handles the original object. The same individual can retain earlier, mutually incompatible object skills. A separate animal evolved part of this ability in gardens where only continued life and material passed to offspring affected reproduction. Its descendants also illustrate how readily evolution can lose a useful skill.

## Biological observations and the mathematical choice

In a human experiment published in 2026, participants learned action–outcome relationships, one outcome lost value, and choices were tested in the absence of further outcomes. This information boundary matters. An animal that first has to try the old action again can succeed by updating its response to the latest reward. Rodent sensory-preconditioning experiments also show that transitions between initially neutral events can support later value-guided behavior. We adopted both experimental ideas. Axion's taste–nutrient remapping is a synthetic analogue with four physically selectable outcomes. [Human outcome devaluation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41691312/) · [Learning transitions between neutral sensory events](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5413864/)

A fly's meal composition changes what it subsequently chooses to eat. The 2025 tachykinin experiments found a pathway through which protein intake suppresses further protein consumption and promotes sugar consumption. This motivated a vector of bodily needs. Four internal storage levels supply four demand spikes. Each genome controls how predicted sensory consequences and current demand contribute to actual muscle competition. [Nutrient-specific appetite experiments](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12198013/)

The design hypothesis is that an organism can reuse a learned physical consequence across several future purposes. A fast, local implementation needs an efference copy of its action, sensory activity it can associate with that action, and sensory events that can modify a second junction even in the absence of action. It can then combine what it predicts with what its body currently needs. The genome specifies local transition tables at both junctions. The circuits develop from inherited constants. The de novo founder plasticity tables initially copy their old state.

For a receptor population, the first bank can express 32 afferents × 4 efferent classes × 4 sensations in sixteen 32-bit words. A second 4 × 4 bit matrix couples these sensations to later inputs from the gut. Each of the four efferent channels yields a four-bit prediction. A learned link is sufficient to propagate an active sensation through the second junction. A signed, inherited two-input table supplies a current for each predicted or unpredicted channel given the active needs. The genome can change the input projection, efference-copy wiring, synaptic truth tables, prior connectivity, direct and indirect gains, novelty current, and influence on somatic motor populations. This includes bypassing either bank and silencing the entire organ. [Neural implementation](../src/outcomes.cuh)

The first and second banks, developmental genes, recent taste, nutrient stores and organic fuel together cost **88 bytes per creature**. At 256-cell capacity the full two-program creature uses 760 bytes of device state. The corresponding individual checkpoint is 784 bytes. Tissue, synaptic updates, decisions, metabolism, interaction and reproduction run on the GPU. The underlying habitat is a partitioned cellular automaton. Alternating local neighborhoods let creatures exchange energy, compete for space, leave stores and alter the sensory conditions later animals encounter. Local plant variants spread through the same shared world. Earlier experiments addressed the connection to continuous artificial life and the limits of a bounded automaton. [Habitat and cellular automata](ecology.md) · [Cell and germline layouts](../include/axion/life.hpp)

## What the animals learned

Each object has a private action-to-flavour permutation. A learner chooses its own actions on 16 objects. In the inferred task, each grasp produces only a flavour. Four passive tastings away from all objects then pair the flavours with four nutrients. We copy the same acquired state into probes of every object under each individual bodily need. Next, every flavour changes nutritional identity. The probes run again immediately and after four new passive samples. The policy only receives raw receptor activity, body signals, and the consequences of its own past actions. The environment consults its private scoring table after the action. Four rotations of the hidden nutrient labels per world exactly counterbalance any successful grasp made without nutrient information. An animal that never grasps can score below 25%. ## Evolution through reproduction

There are two ways to breed these animals. In the explicit learning nursery, performance after sensory experience can affect parentage. Six independently founded populations of 256 animals ran for 384 generations, allowing the whole genome to change. We chose the best of these six on a separate validation stream, then grafted the resulting prospective organ onto the inherited context learner and bred it for 384 generations with only the new organ mutable. The winning integrated population seeded the Prospector experiments. [Initial populations](foraging/selection.csv) · [Integrated populations](foraging/joint.csv) · [Deployable population](../examples/prospector.eco)

In the subsistence nursery each creature meets fruits, acts, pays for its activities, eats what its actions physically yield, and can divide when it has accumulated enough resources. Two complementary conditions use single and paired sensory cues. The selection variables are material actually placed into offspring and a combination of remaining material, offspring and ticks lived. Labels from the cognitive exam never enter reproductive selection or the choice of the published cohort. All genomes and both contact-update tables can mutate. [Solitary growth simulation](../src/subsistence.cuh)

Under severe mineral loss, the cohort with the highest validated offspring biomass reached **48.98%** on held-out passive revaluation in one experiment and **49.64%** in an independent, larger experiment. Each had five sibling cohorts close to chance. The smaller study used 256 candidates for 384 generations and the larger used 1,024 for 1,024. Both used 16 ecological lifetimes per candidate per generation. Milder loss conditions yielded near-chance populations. A further 1,024-generation experiment seeded with a successful grazer produced six cohorts scoring 48.2% to 52.1% on the validation task. In the first external stream the saved grazer chose correctly on 32/64 objects and needs after passive revaluation. These experiments demonstrate partial discovery from bodily consequences. The range of sensory channels, intermediate populations and elementary operations constitutes an engineered inductive bias. ## How much can one observation tell an animal?

The growth-selected grazer's roughly 50% score concealed a different behavior from the Prospector's nearly 100%. In the 16-opportunity population assay, the grazer tried an average of 1.03 distinct grips per object. The Prospector tried 3.96. We tested whether the grazer could make use of the one action it actually experienced.

The external experiment gives an animal zero to four opportunities per object. After its own choices and the passive chemistry samples, we present **every hidden permutation compatible with what that animal observed**. For example, after observing the effect of one grip, six permutations remain possible. We pose all four needs under all six alternatives. The program verifies that changing the hidden scoring table never changes the emitted motor command. Each object and need has equal total weight regardless of how many counterfactuals remain. [Exhaustive public experiment](../tools/outcome-study.py)

With four equally likely effects, a learner that has tried *k* distinct grips can choose the right one for *k* needs. For any other need, each untried grip has probability 1/(4 − *k*) of being right. The highest expected accuracy is therefore 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%, 100% after zero through four distinct observations. At three, the final grip's effect is determined by the three observations. Every score below is from decisions of the deployed CUDA actor. Each row uses 16 independent teaching streams with 16 fresh objects per stream.

| Opportunities per object | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Grazer, selected by growth | 25% | 50% | 50% | 50% | 50% |
| Prospector, selected with extended practice | 25% | 25% | 50% | 75% | 100% |
| Scout, selected with alternating short and long practice | 25% | 50% | 75% | 100% | 100% |

## A shared evolving food web

Each plant carries mutable genes for the appearance and mechanical response of its fruits and a further 32 bits of chemistry. A grasp may release any of four flavours, and the four climates can confer different nutritional effects on the same flavour. The local depletion and mineral inputs, per-nutrient storage capacity and energy budget make bodily needs change with time. The available habitats include complementary materials that themselves supply energy (`balanced`) and complementary materials that allow the oxidation of a separate store of harvested carbon (`food-fuel`). In the latter habitat mineral seepage supplies zero energy. Energy transferred between animals, food, reserves, delayed digestion, external inputs and sinks is checked at census. [Local ecological interactions](../src/life.cu)

The trained prospective circuit had real reproductive value. In eight four-climate worlds with reciprocal starting positions, genetically fixed animals competed with relatives whose prospective currents were disconnected. All **4,998 survivors across 16 contests** had the intact circuitry at tick 2,048. All **6,072 survivors** had intact plasticity in the separate frozen-contact competition. Severing only the learned flavour–nutrient relay gave 5,005 intact versus 1,878 lesioned survivors with ten intact wins in 16 worlds. Reciprocal placement, circuit intervention and zero mutation during these contests isolate the performance of the released phenotype. [Competition records](foraging/duels.csv)

Over longer periods, other traits succeeded. In all four continuously mutating food-fuel worlds in one test, descendants still lived at tick 32,768, while their revaluation assay averaged 20.4%. We checked whether good learners had become rare. At tick 2,048, 4,527 of 7,184 screened residents on four larger islands exceeded 50% and beat both their silenced and frozen-contact controls by more than 12.5 percentage points. Each island still yielded a genotype that scored 100% when retested on 64 fresh worlds with four label rotations. At tick 32,768 none of 8,179 residents passed the same screen. Across four independently founded wild islands, none of 7,854 residents passed at that time. The population fractions describe the initial screen. The held-out tests of the top 16 distinct inherited fingerprints are reported separately. ## Copy fidelity and dispersal

Twelve bits in the existing reproduction gene can encode four separate copying fidelities. An exponent *e* from zero to seven allows variation in one region on one out of every 2^*e* births in expectation. The four regions are somatic programs, body and effectors, synaptic and contextual programs, and prospective programs. The reproduction word itself stays mutable. Checking costs the parent one energy unit per four total exponent points rounded up. Genomes can pay for copying fidelity, give it up, or allocate it unevenly. The `uniform` control uses the rounded mean exponent with the same energetic cost. The `cost-only` control charges the same price while allowing ordinary variation. These controls equalize the cost for the same genome. Individual edit rates can differ.

This freedom alone failed to preserve the released behavior. The Prospector founders had exponents 3, 4, 7, 4 in previously unexpressed bits. Expressing that allocation led to extinction in four of four modular worlds by tick 32,768. Four populations initialized with randomly distributed modifiers survived but averaged 19.7% revaluation under modular inheritance. The single surviving inherited, uniform population scored 50%. Initialization at zero and an independently growth-selected founding population are included. Plant and animal genes remained mutable throughout. [Prospector copying experiment](foraging/fidelity.csv) · [Growth-selected copying experiment](foraging/grazer-fidelity.csv)

An optional ecological passage samples living residents in disjoint spatial-order strata and hatches their germlines in a fresh habitat. Founder abundance reflects survival and reproduction. Parent choice has no access to any cognitive assay. Lineage identifiers and their division counts survive the passage. Each recruit receives fresh bodily resources and untrained neural tissue. Two matched treatments repeat the original vegetation or introduce new producer genomes at every passage. The abiotic landscape, distribution of founding energy, placement of recruits and dispersal key are matched between the treatments. `--settlers` caps the number eligible to contribute, and extinction ends the lineage. The complete sequence runs on the GPU, including sampling, inheritance and regeneration. Only census summaries and explicit saves cross to the host. Separate fitness and common-garden measurements matter because the same variable environment can favor different traits in different populations. [Environmental predictability experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41938214/) · [2026 experimental study of gene flow and plasticity](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13751116/)

Across 128 passages of 256 ticks, all four Prospector populations survived both repeated and novel vegetation. Terminal revaluation averaged 19.0% and 18.9%, respectively. No screened resident exceeded the contact-dependent threshold. Altering the turnover interval to 64 ticks while keeping the total tick budget fixed also failed to sustain the released skill. For the growth-selected grazer, changing the interval affected persistence. All eight repeated and novel vegetation lineages died with 256-tick passages and all eight survived with 64-tick passages. A passage supplies a new founding biomass, so energy across passages is externally subsidized. Per-world energy conservation and across-passage ancestry persistence are checked independently. ## Time to handle a meal

With uninterrupted feeding, grasping new fruit every tick can deliver varied minerals even if the animal always uses the same grip. The optional `--husk 1|2|3` gives foods a processing rate of 16, 8 or 4 units per tick. A mouthful of *m* units occupies the mouth for up to three more ticks, using the integer quotient of *m* minus one by the processing rate. A busy animal can still touch a fruit and sense its flavour. Digestion, movement, social activity and maintenance continue. Two inherited body bits subdivide a bite, exchanging immediate intake for the opportunity to feed sooner. Two spare bits in a pouch word hold the entire per-animal processing state.

We matched four gardens with four fruit variants per property, 128-tick seasons and leaching of three units. With unrestricted animal and plant mutation, the hard-husk worlds recorded 57,860 tastes without ingestion out of 94,747 touches before the last animals died. By tick 2,048 the matched soft worlds had recorded 36,117 out of 370,205. When plants reproduced clonally, one hard-husk population still had 339 animals and 792 divisions of lineage depth at tick 32,768. Residents there chose correctly for 41.0% of bodily needs on their local plants, versus 24.9% after erasing their prospective connections. It was one of four worlds in that treatment. A separate growth-only study produced one cohort with 42.2% held-out revaluation under hard food and otherwise mostly near-chance outcomes. That cohort was identified by its cognitive score after breeding. [Hard fruit](foraging/tough-field.csv) · [Soft fruit](foraging/soft-field.csv) · [Hard-food growth cohorts](foraging/tough-growth.csv) · [Matched soft-food cohorts](foraging/soft-growth.csv) · [Independent cognitive test](foraging/tough-audit.csv)

We searched 27 combinations of seasonal duration, handling time and mineral loss for a fully coevolving world that sustains a measurable benefit from acquired contacts. Each ran on four starting seeds. At 32,768 ticks 57 of 108 worlds had living residents. One had a local choice advantage above five percentage points against both the silenced and reset controls. The best regime by this predeclared criterion was then tested with eight fresh seeds. All eight remained populated at 131,072 ticks. Their actual resident choices pooled to 15.2% correct, versus 18.7% after contact reset and 19.7% with the prospective contribution silenced. These experiments found long-lived populations and a failure of the released phenotype to retain its original advantage. [Every candidate and replication](foraging/niches.csv)

Evolution currently acts within a finite physical and computational vocabulary. A creature can alter its somatic instruction tape, effective tissue size, projection and effector wiring, contact rules and contextual gates, energy allocation, and rates of hereditary variation. The implementation bounds its tissue capacity and tape length, gives it eight motor discharges and specifies what the physical receptors and actuators can do. Four distinct effects form a permutation in the exhaustive Scout test. A world with repeated outcomes, unseen kinds of actuators, or a longer causal chain changes the information available from three examples. The result establishes sample efficiency on this family. It leaves transfer to those other families an empirical question.
