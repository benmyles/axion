# Learning which place a familiar landmark belongs to

Four wings contain visually identical junctions. Each entrance has a private marker. Several intervening observations occur before the animal sees the junction, chooses a grip and experiences the resulting destination. Each animal learns new door effects during its lifetime. The hidden wing controls which physical movements reach each destination. The animal receives receptor spikes, its own motor discharge and the next observation. The wing number and the correct effector are available only to the environment and scorer.

The experiment draws on three observations. Human intracranial recordings during navigation found that remembered objects and their spatial context had distinguishable neural correlates, and that their co-variation predicted behavior. In mice, learning to act in visually ambiguous situations progressively separated hippocampal population representations. A 2026 rat study found a component of population activity that distinguished different goal configurations in the same maze while preserving the alignment of spatial representations. Axion tests one computational inference from these findings. Earlier activity could condition a transition prediction while leaving an acquired spatial map available for other contexts. [Human context-dependent navigation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12453184/) · [Learning under perceptual ambiguity](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11964937/) · [Goal-specific maps in shared space](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77240-6)

## What Wayfarer could already do

Across four independently assigned worlds, an individual hatched from the published Wayfarer population chose correctly on 16/64 questions before practice. When each junction had its own previously unused visual landmark, it reached 64/64 after practice and 64/64 after its doors changed. When the four junctions looked identical, it scored 15/64 after practice and 18/64 after the change. The practice stream gave it two intervening hall observations, and the test gave it four. Reusing an entrance's appearance as its own junction cue gave intermediate results because an additional transition was learned at that same cue. ## Conditional transition learning

The episodic phenotype adds 184 bytes to each animal. Twelve bytes specify hereditary truth tables, charge changes and temporal wiring. A 160-byte bank has one branch per sensory ensemble. Each branch can bind a place and four action-conditioned predictions. Each prediction has a five-bit target and three bits of efficacy. The remaining twelve bytes retain recent observations, a persistent sensory event and a population of cells responsive to disagreement between expected and experienced transitions.

A receptor discharge can remain eligible while other observations occur. On seeing an action's consequence, a branch can respond to a prediction error, bind to the earlier place and change the efficacy or destination of one of its contacts. The inherited program controls recruitment, replacement, potentiation and depression. It also controls which events enter persistent memory. Founders have zero charge changes, so their initial program cannot produce an effective conditional contact. During a choice, responsive branches can change the temporary graph through which spikes propagate. Its ordinary transition contacts remain available across changes of context. Both movement in the shared ecology and the external individual use these operations. [Temporal circuit](../src/temporal.cuh) · [Spiking propagation](../src/wayfinding.cuh) · [CUDA junction environment](../src/junctions.cuh)

The controlled GPU probe starts every wing at the same model state, clock and test noise. The entrance marker varies. The four choices are scored with feedback withheld. Within each private world, balancing the hidden motor permutations gives a history-blind policy an upper bound of one correct wing per goal across the four branches. The separate public sensory-stream experiment advances one real individual continuously across trials. It provides an independent check on the effect in a deployed animal. [Public sensory stream](../tools/passage-study.py)

## Evolution and controls

A breeding generation exposes each candidate to two new environments under four motor rotations. Each candidate's young begin without learned contacts. In a junction nursery, acquisition and relearning affect the number of descendants. The history-only treatments vary three 32-bit words while preserving the parent's body, ordinary map and nutritional learning program. Local breeding uses mating pools of 64 candidates with migration. A separate whole-genome experiment began with random bodies and inert conditional contacts, and allowed every heritable program to change.

- After 64 generations in a repeated-texture corridor, the best of four history-only populations scored 1,855/2,048 revised choices on a fresh set of junctions with eight intervening observations. Hiding the entrance marker or freezing conditional plasticity gave 512/2,048. [Four founding populations](passages/initial-cohorts.csv) · [Causal interventions](passages/initial-audit.csv)
- One of six whole-genome populations reached 7,682/8,192 revised choices across four further test seeds. Hiding the entrance gave 2,041. Swapping the entrance markers while preserving the physical destinations gave 154. Individuals varied substantially. These counts describe the selected population and four rotations of each world. [Independent whole-genome cohorts](passages/wild-breeding.csv) · [Transfer and interventions](passages/wild-audit.csv) · [Population checkpoint](../examples/passage-founders.eco)
- One early line scored 8,026/8,192 revised choices after 128 distractors when plasticity was suspended during the approach. Across four continuously active public individuals it scored 17/64. Branching from two learned checkpoints exposed the cause. The animals started with 32/32 correct action-conditioned contacts. After 32 distracting events with plasticity active, they had 0/32 and made 8/32 correct choices. Freezing just the conditional contacts preserved all 32 and restored 32/32 choices. [Frozen approaches](passages/long-retention.csv) · [Continuous individuals](passages/long-stream.csv) · [Before and after each intervention](passages/recall-diagnosis.csv)

The subsequent circuit lets a branch's existing efficacy influence recruitment by a new place. The attention program can retain the activity of a recognized receptor, and temporary sensory adaptation uses spare bits in each branch. The associative form can assign the same earlier cue to several present places by mixing both addresses. Different pairs can collide in its 32 physical branches. A genotype specifies which of these operations contributes to a choice. The bank size, arithmetic, motor interface, sensory alphabet and environmental physics are design constraints. They set bounds on the behaviors evolution can discover.

Two inheritance errors affected early experiments. A single-site transplant originally transferred the ordinary map and reset the temporal genes. The early distraction and attention cohorts therefore developed from newly initialized temporal programs on their donor bodies. A second error allowed the food organ's genes to drift during history-only breeding. Published Tracker came from a corrected transplant of only the selected 12 temporal gene bytes onto the original Wayfarer body. The common 924 device bytes at hatching agree except for the four-byte site-derived noise seed at offset 164. Regression tests check both inheritance paths. ## Can a cue earn attention through accurate predictions?

A mixed nursery varies both exposure order and texture familiarity. Every two generations include paired approaches, a continuously changing animal, familiar sights and previously unseen sights. All candidates in a generation receive the same two environments and their rotated controls. A mixed audit picks the schedule and texture set independently for each held-out world. The animal receives the same sensory and motor interface in each condition.

Six populations descended from Sentinel and bred for 384 generations under mixed conditions. The selected individual made 23/64 revised choices with unfamiliar sights in a continuous lifetime and 16/64 with familiar sights. In eight fresh 1,024-event branches it scored 8/32 with learning active and 32/32 when only the conditional contacts were frozen during the approach. Mixed rearing alone left interference between experiences. [Population selection](passages/mixed-breeding.csv) · [Independent screen](passages/mixed-screen.csv) · [Continuous recurrence](passages/mixed-continuous.csv) · [Familiar sights](passages/mixed-familiar.csv) · [Long-delay replay](passages/mixed-branches.csv)

The predictive phenotype takes a different approach to the same memory budget. When the animal acts, a conditional branch and the ordinary map may both predict a consequence. After that consequence arrives, the four possible combinations of match and mismatch index four inherited signs. A sign can raise or lower a two-bit local value. Seeing the cue again can recruit it into working memory according to that value and a genetically set threshold. The values occupy the two bits previously used for sensory adaptation, so the phenotype still requires 1,108 bytes of device state. A July 2026 human study found that uncertainty in visual sequences altered anticipatory hippocampal activity and subsequent cortical responses. Using relative prediction success to select a cue is an engineering hypothesis motivated by that observation. [Human intracranial measurements](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-026-02345-6) · [Inherited updates and retrieval](../src/temporal.cuh)

Six matched 768-generation cohorts began with the same Tracker parent and the same seeds, environments, genetic population sizes and mutation opportunities. The predictive phenotype improved both acquisition and revision in all six cohort comparisons. Its selected individual answered 60/64 revised questions and 58/64 revalued questions with 128 unfamiliar distractors recurring across the lifetime. Eight further world streams yielded 106/128 revised and 97/128 revalued choices. Freezing the two-bit updates from birth gave these same totals in the eight worlds. The selected four-bit sign table was 15, so every observed outcome raised the value. Of 96 screened genotypes, 57 had table 15 and 39 had table 11. All sixteen single-locus replacements on the selected body scored at the 25% floor on the first 1,024-event approaches in the two diagnostic maps. These results support a change in the selected population and a role for familiarity. A general advantage from comparing prediction accuracy across cues remains unverified. ## Selection through reproduction

A separate population began with the previously published Nomads. In six 384-generation experiments every heritable program could mutate. Survival, remaining energy and material invested in offspring supplied the breeding score. Each newborn had to feed itself through its own leg and mouth discharges in a private catchment. In the 32-room environment, eight individual landmarks and three shared visual identities represent the 32 locations. Four springs supply food. The physical location remains private to the environment. [Nursery and field kernel](../src/migration.cuh) · [Independent populations](passages/unrestricted-growth.csv)

The population with the highest initial offspring mass showed no advantage from conditional contacts in the subsequent test. After comparing all six populations on four seeds, we selected seed 8933 for confirmation on eight additional seeds. Each condition below comprised 4,096 matched lifetimes. Freezing conditional contacts reduced offspring mass in all eight environments. [Comparison across the six cohorts](passages/resource-cohorts.csv) · [Held-out confirmation](passages/resource-confirmation.csv)

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Animals reaching a spring | 3,570 | 2,676 | 3,556 |
| Full lifetimes survived | 1,984 | 1,707 | 1,986 |
| Births | 20,829 | 17,901 | 20,778 |
| Material passed to offspring | 834,827 | 718,378 | 833,126 |

The intact line found springs in 87.2% of its lifetimes, compared with 65.3% with conditional contacts frozen. At a spring it moved away on 1,376 of 1,589,941 opportunities. On dry terrain it moved on 42,428 of 95,122. A one-event memory recovered nearly the same reproductive yield. Counterfactual tests gave little evidence of navigating along shortest paths to satisfy a specific need. These animals evolved a way to reach and occupy productive sites. Long-delay context memory has separate experimental support in the taught lineages. [Residence, movements and arrivals](passages/residence.csv) · [Founding Reedlings](../examples/reedlings.eco)

Four shared 32 × 32 worlds each ran for 32,768 ticks with evolvable plants and animals. Their final populations were 458, 472, 300 and 352. The deepest surviving lineages were 245, 159, 229 and 175 generations. Across 16 side-swapped competitions the intact treatment beat animals limited to their base map in four, those with conditional plasticity frozen in four, and those limited to the most recent observation in six. ./axion life --load examples/confluence.eco --steps 2048 --report-every 128

The public lesson generator works independently of the CUDA world. In an individual session, `stride` presents a sensory event, `travel` executes a real movement and returns its consequence, `savour` delivers the nutrient at a destination, and `orient` reads a movement at a specified need. Lines beginning with `# phase` group scores on the run's WebP without entering the sensory stream. World plates sample animals and terrain on the GPU. A dense population can hide individual trajectories between sampling instants.

## GPU execution

The 256-cell animals here occupy 1,108 bytes of complete device state. The 1,132-byte standalone individual includes its file header and checksum. In a frozen batch the ordinary and contextual contacts, the pending feedback and the simulated instant are shared by successive questions to the same animal. The kernel projects the graph once into private registers. It then evaluates each question against that graph. A rolling index cycles through the stimulus table. Learning or stepping the animal returns to the event-by-event kernel path. The GPU uses subgroup shuffles, population counts and packed integer operations throughout. [CUDA actor](../src/actor.cuh) · [Temporal projection](../src/temporal.cuh)

The Blackwell laptop benchmark used 1,152 distinct source, destination and need combinations with a primed, trained Tracker. The serial actor supplied the reference physical discharges for the throughput check. Each replica owned a full state allocation and answered 64 queries. At 65,536 replicas, the median of 12 CUDA event measurements was **182.53 million decisions/s**, up from **104.47 million/s** before graph reuse and circular indexing. All choices and fingerprints agreed. The optimized kernel used 249 registers and reported zero local-memory bytes per thread. These scores measure execution equivalence and exclude host transfers and journal rendering. [Measurements for all three batch sizes](passages/inference.csv)

A full 1,024 × 1,024 basin began with **1,048,576 animals** and a plant at each site. Sixteen ticks produced 16,190,945,280 expressed somatic cell updates and ended with 652,206 live animals. The median over four runs was **49.83 billion cell updates/s**. Each tick also advanced local resources, individual neural state, plant and animal metabolism, movement and reproduction. Explicit resident simulation buffers occupied **1,228,210,772 bytes**. This count excludes the driver, machine code, transient sampling buffers and renderer. The four runs produced the same world fingerprint. [World size, occupancy, GPU times and allocations](passages/field-scale.csv)

```sh
tools/passage-study.py scale --animal examples/trackers.eco --navigation associative --seeds 1847 --repetitions 4
tools/passage-study.py inference --animal examples/tracker.mind --repetitions 4
```

The measured behaviors concern 32 sensory ensembles, four tested motor choices and private finite worlds. Population selection and individual screening favor good learners in these environments. Shared worlds also underwent founder sweeps. The evidence establishes specific forms of within-lifetime adaptation, conditional prediction and transfer. Human-child-level general learning and sustained open-ended increases in complexity remain unverified.
