# Learning from a maker who has left

A pupil can acquire two conflicting conventions, identify which one a piece of fired clay preserves, then use that memory to interpret a stranger’s reply. Each community develops its native calls during paid physical encounters. The human names *willow* and *alder* enter later through demonstrations. The field habitat and the controlled workshop give very different results.

## Why change the model?

Thirty-two shared practice opportunities were sufficient for newborn copies from early Reedbed to make 85.3% productive grips on a fresh four-way test. The same opportunities produced 30.7% with newborns from the later Soundings population. Both inherited learning ability and individual experience mattered. A median late resident had made nine native contacts. Its writable organ had a single set of weights for every partner. [Practice, erasure and independently reared partners](clay/practice.csv)

In human recordings, different medial temporal neurons carried information about a picture and the context of the comparison. A November 2026 human study found that transient hippocampal stimulation impaired inference that depended on a learned relational structure. The engineering hypothesis here is that evidence about one association can reinstate a larger set of associations. The model uses a few competing spiking assemblies and measures whether that inference transfers to a different physical property. The recordings and intervention motivate the question. The discrete gates and thresholds are engineering choices. [Human single-unit recordings](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09910-2) · [Human relational structure learning and inference](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-026-02627-w)

Wild chimpanzees have opportunities to learn by watching several tolerant partners. Field observations at Dindefelo also documented tool recoveries and tolerated transfers. An infant study examined how observed grasps transfer to unfamiliar tools. Axion gives a young animal access to a held or deposited object, charges for its own movements and lets it experience what its own hand does. These observations motivated separate interventions on access, proprioception and the information carried by the object. [Role models and access](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-025-07885-4) · [Tool transfers at Dindefelo](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42819407/) · [Infant grasps of novel tools](https://doi.org/10.1111/infa.70126)

## What the material can do

A grain has eight impression sites. A pressure contact in wet clay can retain a relief, a resonance and a physical trace of the outcome. The relief can be inspected without hearing its pitch. Sounding a groove costs movement. Repressing the same site replaces its impression. The representation occupies 12 bytes per carrier, including the time of its youngest alteration. That single timestamp gives a conservative certificate when an entire object predates its reader. The creature must infer a maker from these physical observations.

The impression follows its grain through a lift, set, push, pull, construction bout, death drop and hand-to-hand transfer. A newborn can occupy a site containing someone else’s cargo. Its own learned associations begin empty. A donor with a limb and a willing recipient with an empty hand can exchange the grain, paying one energy unit apiece. Shelter, surface composition and water flow govern abrasion. Fired material resists it. A multi-finger sweep can sound several occupied grooves on a reachable object. The sweep costs one energy unit plus one per three additional contacts. Body shape, inherited expression and the available surface determine how many contacts occur.

## How one brain retains several conventions

Each animal can express one to four context assemblies. Their maximum number and recruitment threshold are heritable. New observations can recruit another assembly during life. Each has independent writable projections from material to sound and from receptive property to query, with reciprocal use for hearing. The hand’s proprioceptive mapping is shared across contexts. A resident therefore needs to calibrate its own hand even when it has recovered another animal’s acoustic conventions.

An assembly predicts a set of possible pitches for a visible relief. Agreement, disagreement and the number of tied responses determine the current into its four-bit activity state. The signed increments, coloring mask, competition, persistence, reporting threshold, finger gates and stroke routing come from genes. The inferred assembly can change after hearing one groove while the underlying contact strengths remain fixed. A subsequent question can then use another property or the other projection in that assembly. Recolored artifacts, missing sounds and an intervention fixing the activity state test these dependencies.

During a diagnostic probe the creature can see different reliefs before it strikes. It can favor one on which candidate assemblies disagree. The tested filter is excluded. Pitch enters the decision only after a paid stroke. During tuition the learner can also touch ordinary surface and hand combinations. The pupil has no access to the actual community identity in either inference or speech production.

The implementation fixes the possible sensory channels, the number of grooves, the available sounds and an upper bound on neural assemblies. Evolution changes the organisms operating within those bounds. Whether the animals can invent a new representational medium, expand this grammar or sustain increasingly complex traditions remains an empirical question.

## Matched selection and four generations of artifacts

Four independent nursery seeds each evaluated two reproductive regimes. A learner spends a common provision on touching its own hand and two communities’ artifacts. It subsequently shares food with adults during 128 opportunities for contact on changing ground. Its remaining reserve can fund eggs at 16 units apiece. In the proportional condition funded eggs determine reproductive weight. In the uniform control, parents that could afford eggs share parental access evenly. Both use the same native germline pool. All held challenge communities use new seeds.

## Do ordinary objects transmit anything in the field?

Two isogenic reference speakers acquired different native calls during independent lives. We separately introduced their acoustic histories into otherwise matched occupied habitats. After 128 ordinary watershed ticks, we removed every animal and transferred only the soil and its surviving ground objects to fresh hatchlings. Both sets of hatchlings received the same germline sources. Direct native work and the airborne audience were closed. The experiment compared each receiver with both original reference speakers under identical fresh physical substitutions.

| First 16 ticks on inherited ground | With the matching speaker | With the other speaker | Opportunities per comparison |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Single contact | 358,168 | 354,857 | 1,422,016 |
| Reachable grooves and voluntary transfers | 358,359 | 341,226 | 1,378,944 |
| Same reach, pitches cycled on each object | 348,773 | 343,397 | 1,378,944 |
| Same reach, impressions smoothed | 346,110 | 346,110 | 1,384,576 |
| Same reach, own hand practice closed | 379,553 | 379,553 | 1,518,336 |

The matched-speaker advantage was 0.23 percentage points for single contacts and 1.24 points with broader access. It vanished when impressions were removed or the hatchlings could not calibrate their hands. The arms can have different population sizes because movement and survival consume actual reserves. These are four independent founding seeds and many paired physical alternatives inside each seed. The broad field effect is modest compared with repeated exposure to a consistent object in a workshop. ## Continuing Soundings

Five continuations began from the existing Soundings checkpoint at tick 95,616. They expanded from 128 × 128 to 256 × 256 after 1,024 ticks and ended 32,768 ticks after release. The main run introduced only the new context germlines into existing residents. Their native acoustic projections, somatic network, material skills and body plans came from Soundings. Their descendants mutated and reproduced within the watershed.

The shared-object continuation ended with **23,724 animals, 1,365 body forms and 1,528,832 expressed somatic spiking cells** at generation 1,524. There were 31,293 acquired context assemblies, with a maximum of four in one animal. The watershed retained 225,482 impressions and had recorded 98,218 hand-to-hand transfers. Its readers recovered 8,656,097 impressions whose youngest alteration preceded the reader’s birth. The original substrate continuation ended with 22,278 residents and 2,213 body forms. One matched ancestry supplies these comparisons.

Only two of 27,398 late neighboring pairs reached 75% on the native filter screen. Recovery from actual late artifacts also gave little evidence of a stable community-wide convention. Blindly sampled old objects can contain conflicting or irrelevant calls. These measurements distinguish persistent physical storage from preserving a useful code. ## A conversation about a hidden source

The released pupil was selected from 1,024 candidates on separate physical screening tables. The screen favored productive interactions with both speakers and sensitivity to exchanging the specimens. Both adult reference speakers have identical acoustic genes. Separate developmental histories produced their different conventions. The pupil’s word-learning rule was selected separately in a visual language nursery. Its acoustic conventions and human words have different training histories.

The teacher provided 432 captions at visible, paid encounters and 168 witnessed question-and-answer demonstrations. The pupil sees the teacher’s sounds, both local percepts and, when resolved, its own active context cells. It can bind words to any of these coactive sources. In a question demonstration the teacher also supplies one observed answer word. Axion can then infer which changeable constituent a complete question requests. The text of a question, its word order and the source names come from the demonstrations. The human word bank has 32 forms and retains 48 recent sentence examples. The host passes sound patterns to the device, and keeps the independent expected answer outside the CUDA inputs.

The four property–appearance pairs on the diagonal were excluded from tuition. The material questions also withheld the word *rim* in the property position of a demonstrated question and *ochre* in the appearance position of its inverse. Both were available in ordinary captions. The following outcomes include those recombinations.

| What the pupil was asked | Matching object, one tap | Pupil chooses up to four taps | Other object, same stopping rule | Return voice shuttered, same stopping rule |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Who worked this clay? | 0 / 384 | 336 / 384 | 0 / 384 | 336 / 384 |
| What did the named property yield? | 384 / 384 | 384 / 384 | 64 / 384 | 0 / 384 |
| Where did the named appearance occur? | 384 / 384 | 384 / 384 | 64 / 384 | 0 / 384 |

One stroke could reinstate a useful native mapping while falling below the confidence threshold for reporting a maker. Under the self-directed condition, the pupil made 864 taps for 384 source questions, gave 336 correct names and withheld its remaining 48 answers. A covered foreign object elicited 336 wrong names. The live material facts required the current speaker’s return sound. A maker’s name could be recovered from the artifact with that return path shuttered. All 384 incorrect material assertions in the matching self-directed condition received silence. An independently taught register reversed the two source names, changed the word order and used new material words. It recovered the newly assigned name on 336 of 384 source questions. The pupil without external word tuition answered zero of 128. [Factorial language results](clay/dialogue.csv) · [English transcripts](clay/stream-utterances.csv.gz) · [Renamed sources and changed word orders](clay/heath-utterances.csv.gz) · [Untaught words](clay/untaught-utterances.csv.gz)

In a live exchange the same pupil said *willow* after two taps, then *opal* in response to a material question without another tap. It withheld a source name when asked who had observed a contradictory material outcome. With another visitor and its object, it said *alder* after two taps and subsequently identified the *ridge*. This demonstrates bounded inference about provenance, an unseen material feature and a previously witnessed question form. General language, independently invented grammar and human-child-level cognition remain open goals.

## Device representation and throughput

The optional field adds 48 bytes of writable context tissue, a 12-byte clay coating on the ground and space for a 12-byte carried coating per site. The writable native and context circuits together occupy 104 bytes per slot. A scalar kernel can compute every projection directly from those states. For long batches with one fixed pupil, 16 bytes cache the four sets of public pitch predictions.

For heterogeneous inference, a 64-byte frozen representation packs those forward winners, the inverse projections, shared joint and voice wiring, context gates, colors and current activity. It retains every tie and the ability to change active context. The ordinary transfer study uses the frozen projections across repeated private-source trials. Online ecology retains the writable circuits. A complete frozen inference step chooses a visible groove, hears its pitch, changes context activity, reinstates a map and emits native motor and vocal decisions. The result trace includes the chosen relief, pitch, selected assembly, membrane levels, incoming-sound interpretation, emitted query and reply, and grip.

On the laptop’s RTX PRO 2000 Blackwell GPU, the 20,367-resident benchmark gave **1.276 ms writable and 0.786 ms frozen** for 1,048,576 decisions. All 8,388,608 ordered traces in the eight batches agreed. That is **1.33 billion frozen decisions/s** for this kernel and working set. A smaller roster of 3,290 residents gave 1.62 billion/s over batches of 2,097,152. These device-event timings cover the choice kernel. A forward-JIT build of the acoustic and habitat kernels from SM 80 PTX produced identical expanded habitat bytes and 68 matching field and intervention measurements after 193 ticks. The independent tests also reconstructed the pupil’s strike choices and source activity from checkpoint bytes. The whole expanded shared-object population advanced at 62.2 million site-ticks/s during its recorded last 24,576 ticks. A `.clay` collection stores two physical objects, their glazes, an observation time and an integrity checksum. In a reed session, `/kiln FILE covered` hides their coloration and preserves access to the reliefs, `/knock N` buys up to N reference strikes and `/study-clay N` offers a sequence of self-movements with the material. `/clay-inquiry N` sets a ceiling for self-directed evidence collection. `/show-query QUESTION | ANSWER` needs a witnessed encounter and a uniquely grounded observed answer. `/reed-visitor FILE` brings another artist into the same session while retaining the pupil’s state.
